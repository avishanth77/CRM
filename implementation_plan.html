1. Executive Summary & Core Architectural Principles

The TEXOL Configurable Problem-Led Discovery (PLD) CRM is an enterprise B2B SaaS platform engineered from first principles to enforce a disciplined, problem-first commercial qualification methodology. Traditional CRMs manage contacts and linear sales pipelines without understanding whether an authentic business problem exists, whether that problem has measurable financial impact, or whether specific product capabilities can resolve it.

The First Law of TEXOL CRM Architecture: The CRM Core is 100% generic. Products are data configurations, never hardcoded code branches or product-specific database tables. VanForces™ is the first product configuration. All subsequent TEXOL software products (TaskForces, EntAura, FleetForces) configure their modules, capabilities, ICP rules, questionnaires, and pipeline gates entirely through metadata versioning without writing a single line of backend code or running a schema migration.

1.1 The Five Architectural Pillars

  • Universal Generic Commercial Core: Accounts, Contacts, Leads, Opportunities, Activities, and Partners reside in an immutable generic core schema. Dynamic attributes are modeled through typed EAV/JSONB attribute models (account_attribute).
  • Versioned Configuration Governance: Every product qualification questionnaire, ICP knock-out rule, scoring model, and stage gate is bound to an immutable ProductConfigurationVersion. Every completed qualification assessment is pinned to a specific version ID for complete historical auditability and reproducibility.
  • Multi-Product First-Class Citizen: A single customer organization can simultaneously be an active Customer for VanForces, an Evaluation candidate for TaskForces ERP, and a Prospect for EntAura AI. A single commercial Opportunity supports multi-product line items.
  • Problem-Led Progression with Mandatory Gates: Progression between lifecycle stages is strictly gated. A high questionnaire score can never qualify a lead if a confirmed business problem is missing. Discovery cannot complete without mapping every confirmed problem to at least one product capability.
  • Integrated Enterprise Boundary: Built-in bi-directional ERP master data synchronization (SAP S/4HANA, Odoo, MS Dynamics), omni-channel messaging adapters (Email, WhatsApp Cloud), multi-touch attribution (First, Last, Linear, W-Shaped), and EntAura AI contextual integration.

6. ICP Engine & PLD Scoring Architecture

6.1 ICP Evaluation vs. PLD Scoring Distinction

A critical architectural principle of the TEXOL specification is that ICP (Ideal Customer Profile) is strictly a stage gate, NOT a dimension of the PLD Score:

  • ICP Check determines whether an organization qualifies demographically and operationally for a given product (e.g., industry, number of vans, field sales model). It outputs a binary Knockout pass/fail and a 0-100% Fit Score. If a knockout rule fails, the lead is disqualified outright.
  • PLD Qualification evaluates the commercial viability of an active sales project across 6 universal dimensions. It evaluates Problem Severity, Business Value, Decision Access, Timeline, Technical Fit, and Commercial Potential.
The Missing Problem Hard Rule: A high score can never qualify a lead if the required business problem is missing. If a lead has a calculated score of 70-100% but 0 confirmed business problems are attached to the record, the classification is automatically capped at DEVELOPMENT.
